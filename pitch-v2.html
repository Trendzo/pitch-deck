06  The door moment

How try-and-buy works

The sale closes at the door.

A waited trial is not a per-drop job The agent stands at the door for the whole trial. Per-drop pay pushes against waiting; a salary does not.
Returned stock needs a custodian What comes back is still the store's goods. There is no warehouse to absorb it — the same agent carries it back.
The door moment is the product Not a delivery step. Salaried agents, assigned by zone, can be trained on it and held to it.
All three apps are built and wired to live dispatch. Nothing has launched yet.

07  Why we win

Against everyone else

Most hold three of these. The last two are the ones a warehouse cannot buy.

The row above ours is the closest: metro fast-fashion players already delivering inside an hour, and Slikk already letting the rider wait while you try. But all of them sell stock they bought and hold in their own dark stores, and that is what ties them to metro density. We sell what the shop already owns, so a new city is signed, not built.

Positioning per each company’s published service terms, August 2026 · “Beyond metros” means live service in Tier 2 cities, not stated expansion plans

08  Why now

Why now

The category is early. The expensive lesson has already been paid for by somebody else.

Grocery proved people will pay for speed, then ran out of room. Fashion has the demand and none of the coverage — and the capital-heavy way of serving it has already been tried, in cities we are not starting in.

Commission led. The shop earns as if the discount never happened.

Three streams, one order · a Rs 700 express order on Year-1 costs

Rs 700 value · +Rs 140 commission · +Rs 29 delivery · −Rs 84 discount we fund · −Rs 12 processing · −Rs 57 fleet  =  +Rs 16 contribution

Blended Year 1 is about −Rs 2 an order, positive in Year 2, +Rs 95 by Year 5. CAC Rs 184, LTV Rs 685, 3.7× at maturity.
